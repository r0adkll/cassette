# 0017: iOS

Date: 2026-10-18 · Status: accepted; background capture proposed until the ride test

Milestone 3 ([roadmap](../roadmap.md)): an iPhone app. Decision 0007 already shares everything up to the
screens; this record covers what iOS adds, from a Core Bluetooth spike on an iPhone 14 Pro with our bike
(two bench runs, 2026-10-18; the spike lives on the `spike/ios-corebluetooth` branch).

## Scope

- **First:** the app (every shared screen), notifications and background capture.
- **Then:** WidgetKit widgets, a Live Activity while a refresh runs, and an Apple Watch app. Each is a
  Swift target, and each gets its mockups before it's built ([0006](0006-design-process.md)).
- **Not on iOS:** App updates (TestFlight updates the app), the Quick Settings tile (the Control Center
  equivalent isn't planned), the Wear panel, and the widget and tile tips until widgets exist.

## Look

The same design system ([0012](0012-visual-direction.md)) with iOS behaviours: swipe back from the edge,
the system share sheet, iOS wording for permissions. Window size classes already lay out an iPad or the
iPhone foldable ([0014](0014-large-screens.md)).

**Liquid Glass (iteration 14, G1, approved 2026-10-18; iOS only):** the top bar's circle buttons (back,
close, settings, add bike, the sheet's close, previous and next: every `IconCircleButton`) float over the
content as glass while it scrolls beneath them. Refresh and the other pill buttons stay solid ink, so the
one primary action keeps its contrast over any status colour. Android keeps the solid circles. Rejected:
G2 (Refresh in tinted glass loses contrast over amber and red) and G3 (one floating action bar, which moves
Settings and Add bike from where Android has them).

Drawn natively (prototype, 2026-10-18): `GlassIconButton` puts a UIKit glass button
(`UIButtonConfiguration.glassButtonConfiguration`) in a `UIKitView` placed as an overlay, above the
Compose canvas, so the system samples and refracts the Compose content scrolling under it; before iOS
26 it's the ultra-thin material with a light rim and shadow. The Lucide icon is drawn into a template
image the button tints. Compared with Kyant0's backdrop and FletchMcKee's liquid (Skia shaders, close
imitations; flicker on physical iPhones, an unreleased CMP 1.12 build) and Haze (frosted only), it is
the only option that is iOS 26 glass, with no new dependency; Haze is the fallback if native fails.

- CMP 1.12 doesn't deliver touches to overlay interop views, so the glass only draws: a Compose
  `clickable` with the label and button role takes the tap, and a press scales the glass down a little.
- Overlay views sit above all Compose content and don't follow Compose's alpha, so a screen on its
  way out kept its glass over the incoming one. The nav decoration marks the active screen
  (`LocalTopBarShown`); the others hide their glass at once, and the incoming screen's slides in
  with it. Nothing in Compose can draw over the glass.

**Floating top bars, both apps (2026-10-18):** every screen's top-bar buttons float over its
content (`FloatingTopBar`, `TopBarButton` in `:common:compose`), so scrolling never takes them away;
text beside them (the wordmark, "SET UP", the bike's name) stays in the content and scrolls. Android
keeps its solid circles; iOS draws them as glass. Screens draw edge to edge
([0016](0016-edge-to-edge.md)), so a floating bar pads by the system bars as its scroll column does,
and the content scrolls under both the bar and the status bar.
- Checked on the Simulator (iOS 26.2 light and dark, iOS 18.5): glass over scrolling Diagnostics, taps,
  the UI test. Not yet: a physical iPhone, and the look during push and pop transitions.

**iOS-only screens (iteration 14, approved as drawn):** Bluetooth is named "Bluetooth", and iOS's own
prompt carries our usage string; a blocked permission points to Settings → Cassette. Settings → Capture
adds an "On iPhone" panel: iOS decides when Cassette listens and may miss a short stop; swiping the app
away stops background capture until it's opened again; when the bike was last heard. Notifications are
iOS's: the pre-ride nudge is Time Sensitive with "Snooze until tomorrow" when pressed open.

## What Core Bluetooth gives us (spike)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| The RD's two `0x0933` structures | **Merged** into one manufacturer blob (`0000,0400,0101` or `…0402`). Callbacks that include the scan response carry `0000,0400` with the `FE51` service data. The battery is in every RD callback |
| Frame shapes | RD and Quarq as on Android, all listing `FE51`, so an `FE51` filter catches them. A nameless, serial-less device sent coin-cell battery records (2559 mV, soc 3); which one is open. No shifter or FD beacons were heard |
| Identity | No BLE address: `CBPeripheral.identifier` is a UUID per phone and app. It stands in for the address everywhere (`component.address` holds it as text). Stable within the day; across days and battery swaps is untested |
| GATT, unpaired | The RD's hub tables, `fe54`, `fe56`, `fe58`, `d905000a` and `2a19` all read without the session; the Quarq's `2a19` too. 1 to 14 s from connect to last read, mostly the connect. Discover only the services we read: discovering all 20 of the RD's ran past 15 s |
| Background | With an `FE51` filter and the `bluetooth-central` mode, discoveries keep coming (RD every 1 to 10 s on the bench). They lack the scan response: battery, but no service data, so no serial |
| Relaunch | After the process is killed, iOS relaunches it in the background within seconds and restores the scan (`willRestoreState`). The launch options' Bluetooth key was empty; the restore callback is the signal. Swiping the app away still stops all of this, as iOS documents |
| Background GATT | A read that started in the foreground finished in the background. One started from a background discovery is untested |

## Bluetooth

`IosAdvertScanner` and `IosGattClient` in `:data:ble` (`iosMain`, Kotlin over `platform.CoreBluetooth`)
implement the common interfaces, as 0007 planned. One `CBCentralManager` for the app, created at launch
with a restore identifier so iOS can hand it back.

- **Live:** no service filter, duplicates allowed. Manufacturer data is already merged, so the decoder
  gets the payload without walking AD structures.
- **Addresses:** the identifier's UUID string. Frames without a serial resolve by it, as on Android.
- **GATT:** connect by identifier (`retrievePeripheralsWithIdentifiers`), discover only the services
  holding the characteristics asked for, read, disconnect. Never write.
- **Permissions:** Bluetooth is one ask (`CBManager.authorization`): not determined is Askable, denied or
  restricted is Blocked. Notifications as on Android.

## Background capture (proposed)

`startBackground` scans for `FE51` with state restoration. iOS sets the cadence, so the scan profiles all
map to the same scan; Diagnostics says so. Discoveries go to the same `BackgroundDeliveryHandler` as
Android. Because background frames carry no serial, a component is matched by its identifier, learnt
from foreground frames or a hub read (`fe54` is the serial). When the hub is heard in the background
and hasn't been read for a while, read its tables, as decision 0009 proposes on Android.

Open until the ride test (spike questions 4, 6 and 8): how often a locked phone hears the bike on a
ride, whether a hub read can start from a background discovery, and whether identifiers survive a day,
a battery swap and a reboot. If they don't, the identifier is re-learnt in the foreground.

The daily not-seen reminders run from a `BGAppRefreshTask`, which iOS schedules as it sees fit, so
they may arrive late.

## Notifications

`IosNotifier` posts the same three alerts as `AndroidNotifier` through `UNUserNotificationCenter`, with
string identifiers built from the Android ids (`battery-<component>`, `pre-ride-<bike>`,
`not-seen-<bike>`), so a newer alert replaces the older one. iOS has no channels; the pre-ride nudge is
time-sensitive. "Snooze until tomorrow" is a notification action. Taps open the same `cassette://`
links ([0011](0011-onboarding-alerts-and-data.md)).

## App

`:app:ios` (Kotlin, iOS targets only) holds `IosAppGraph` and the `ComposeUIViewController` that hosts
Circuit, and builds the `CassetteKit` framework. `app/ios/Cassette` is the Xcode project, generated by
XcodeGen from `project.yml` (the `.xcodeproj` isn't committed): a small SwiftUI app with the app
delegate (restoration, notifications, background tasks) and the framework. iOS 17 or later.

- Bundle ids: `com.r0adkll.cassette` for TestFlight builds, `com.r0adkll.cassette.debug` for local
  debug builds, so both can be on a phone.
- Signing team `N6RWL4DC5S`. Build numbers are the commit count, like Android's.
- Diagnostics: the capture log, raw adverts and hub tables as on Android ([0005](0005-diagnostics.md));
  the bundle goes to the share sheet. The Wear panel is hidden (no Data Layer).

## Distribution

TestFlight (internal testers), uploaded from CI with an App Store Connect API key, alongside the
Android builds' Drop uploads ([0008](0008-tooling.md)). CI's `iOS app` job builds the app and runs the
Simulator UI test on every push and pull request; `TestFlight` archives the Release build on pushes
to main and uploads it by exporting the archive (`ExportOptions.plist`), with automatic, cloud-managed
signing through the API key. It needs the app created in App Store Connect for `com.r0adkll.cassette`
and an API key (App Manager or Admin) in `APP_STORE_CONNECT_KEY_ID`, `APP_STORE_CONNECT_ISSUER_ID` and
`APP_STORE_CONNECT_KEY_BASE64`; without them it skips, as the Drop upload does without its keystore.

## Later surfaces

Widgets, the Live Activity and the Watch read one snapshot of the bikes, the same one the phone sends
to Wear ([0013](0013-wear.md)): JSON written to an App Group for widgets and the Live Activity, and sent
over WatchConnectivity to the watch. The details are decided with their mockups.
