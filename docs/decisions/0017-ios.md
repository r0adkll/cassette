# 0017: iOS

Date: 2026-10-18 · Status: accepted; background capture proposed until the ride test

Milestone 3 ([roadmap](../roadmap.md)): an iPhone app. Decision 0007 already shares everything up to the
screens; this record covers what iOS adds, from a Core Bluetooth spike on an iPhone 14 Pro with our bike
(two bench runs, 2026-10-18). The spike app is deleted; its findings are below, and its remaining
questions are answered with the app itself (Diagnostics' ride tests and capture log).

## Scope

- **First:** the app (every shared screen), notifications and background capture.
- **Then:** WidgetKit widgets, a Live Activity while a refresh runs, and an Apple Watch app. Each is a
  Swift target, and each gets its mockups before it's built ([0006](0006-design-process.md)).
- **Not on iOS:** App updates (TestFlight updates the app), the Quick Settings tile (the Control Center
  equivalent isn't planned), the Wear panel (Diagnostics shows the Apple Watch in its place), and the
  widget and tile tips (an app can't add a widget to the iOS home screen).

## Look

The same design system ([0012](0012-visual-direction.md)) with iOS behaviours: swipe back from the edge,
the system share sheet, iOS wording for permissions. Window size classes already lay out an iPad or the
iPhone foldable ([0014](0014-large-screens.md)).

**Liquid Glass (iteration 14, G1, approved 2026-10-18; iOS only):** the top bar's circle buttons (back,
close, settings, add bike, the sheet's close, previous and next: every `IconCircleButton`) float over the
content as glass while it scrolls beneath them. Refresh and the other pill buttons stay solid ink, so the
one primary action keeps its contrast over any status colour. Android keeps the solid circles. Rejected:
G2 (Refresh in tinted glass loses contrast over amber and red) and G3 (one floating action bar, which moves
Settings and Add bike from where Android has them).

Drawn natively (prototype, 2026-10-18): `GlassIconButton` puts a UIKit glass button
(`UIButtonConfiguration.glassButtonConfiguration`) in a `UIKitView` placed as an overlay, above the
Compose canvas, so the system samples and refracts the Compose content scrolling under it; before iOS
26 it's the ultra-thin material with a light rim and shadow. The Lucide icon is drawn into a template
image the button tints. Compared with Kyant0's backdrop and FletchMcKee's liquid (Skia shaders, close
imitations; flicker on physical iPhones, an unreleased CMP 1.12 build) and Haze (frosted only), it is
the only option that is iOS 26 glass, with no new dependency; Haze is the fallback if native fails.

- CMP 1.12 doesn't deliver touches to overlay interop views, so the glass only draws: a Compose
  `clickable` with the label and button role takes the tap, and a press scales the glass down a little.
- Overlay views sit above all Compose content and don't follow Compose's alpha, so a screen on its
  way out kept its glass over the incoming one. The nav decoration marks the active screen
  (`LocalTopBarShown`); the others hide their glass at once, and the incoming screen's slides in
  with it. Nothing in Compose can draw over the glass.

**Floating top bars, both apps (2026-10-18):** every screen's top-bar buttons float over its
content (`FloatingTopBar`, `TopBarButton` in `:common:compose`), so scrolling never takes them away;
text beside them (the wordmark, "SET UP", the bike's name) stays in the content and scrolls. Android
keeps its solid circles; iOS draws them as glass. Screens draw edge to edge
([0016](0016-edge-to-edge.md)), so a floating bar pads by the system bars as its scroll column does,
and the content scrolls under both the bar and the status bar.
- Checked on the Simulator (iOS 26.2 light and dark, iOS 18.5): glass over scrolling Diagnostics, taps,
  the UI test. Not yet: a physical iPhone, and the look during push and pop transitions.

**iOS-only screens (iteration 14, approved as drawn):** Bluetooth is named "Bluetooth", and iOS's own
prompt carries our usage string; a blocked permission points to Settings → Cassette. Settings → Capture
adds an "On iPhone" panel: iOS decides when Cassette listens and may miss a short stop; swiping the app
away stops background capture until it's opened again; when the bike was last heard. Notifications are
iOS's: the pre-ride nudge is Time Sensitive with "Snooze until tomorrow" when pressed open.

## What Core Bluetooth gives us (spike)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| The RD's two `0x0933` structures | **Merged** into one manufacturer blob (`0000,0400,0101` or `…0402`). Callbacks that include the scan response carry `0000,0400` with the `FE51` service data. The battery is in every RD callback |
| Frame shapes | RD and Quarq as on Android, all listing `FE51`, so an `FE51` filter catches them. A nameless, serial-less device sent coin-cell battery records (2559 mV, soc 3); which one is open. No shifter or FD beacons were heard |
| Identity | No BLE address: `CBPeripheral.identifier` is a UUID per phone and app. It stands in for the address everywhere (`component.address` holds it as text). Stable within the day; across days and battery swaps is untested |
| GATT, unpaired | The RD's hub tables, `fe54`, `fe56`, `fe58`, `d905000a` and `2a19` all read without the session; the Quarq's `2a19` too. 1 to 14 s from connect to last read, mostly the connect. Discover only the services we read: discovering all 20 of the RD's ran past 15 s |
| Background | With an `FE51` filter and the `bluetooth-central` mode, discoveries keep coming (RD every 1 to 10 s on the bench). They lack the scan response: battery, but no service data, so no serial |
| Relaunch | After the process is killed, iOS relaunches it in the background within seconds and restores the scan (`willRestoreState`). The launch options' Bluetooth key was empty; the restore callback is the signal. Swiping the app away still stops all of this, as iOS documents |
| Background GATT | A read that started in the foreground finished in the background. One started from a background discovery is untested |

## Bluetooth

`IosAdvertScanner` and `IosGattClient` in `:data:ble` (`iosMain`, Kotlin over `platform.CoreBluetooth`)
implement the common interfaces, as 0007 planned. One `CBCentralManager` for the app, created at launch
with a restore identifier so iOS can hand it back.

- **Live:** no service filter, duplicates allowed. Manufacturer data is already merged, so the decoder
  gets the payload without walking AD structures.
- **Addresses:** the identifier's UUID string. Frames without a serial resolve by it, as on Android.
- **GATT:** connect by identifier (`retrievePeripheralsWithIdentifiers`), discover only the services
  holding the characteristics asked for, read, disconnect. Never write.
- **Permissions:** Bluetooth is one ask (`CBManager.authorization`): not determined is Askable, denied or
  restricted is Blocked. Notifications as on Android.

## Background capture (proposed)

`startBackground` scans for `FE51` with state restoration. iOS sets the cadence, so the scan profiles all
map to the same scan; Diagnostics says so. Discoveries go to the same `BackgroundDeliveryHandler` as
Android. Because background frames carry no serial, a component is matched by its identifier, learnt
from foreground frames or a hub read (`fe54` is the serial). When the hub is heard in the background
and hasn't been read for a while, read its tables, as decision 0009 proposes on Android.

Open until the ride test (spike questions 4, 6 and 8): how often a locked phone hears the bike on a
ride, whether a hub read can start from a background discovery, and whether identifiers survive a day,
a battery swap and a reboot. If they don't, the identifier is re-learnt in the foreground.

The daily not-seen reminders run from a `BGAppRefreshTask`, which iOS schedules as it sees fit, so
they may arrive late.

## Notifications

`IosNotifier` posts the same three alerts as `AndroidNotifier` through `UNUserNotificationCenter`, with
string identifiers built from the Android ids (`battery-<component>`, `pre-ride-<bike>`,
`not-seen-<bike>`), so a newer alert replaces the older one. iOS has no channels; the pre-ride nudge is
time-sensitive. "Snooze until tomorrow" is a notification action. Taps open the same `cassette://`
links ([0011](0011-onboarding-alerts-and-data.md)).

## App

`:app:ios` (Kotlin, iOS targets only) holds `IosAppGraph` and the `ComposeUIViewController` that hosts
Circuit, and builds the `CassetteKit` framework. `app/ios/Cassette` is the Xcode project, generated by
XcodeGen from `project.yml` (the `.xcodeproj` isn't committed): a small SwiftUI app with the app
delegate (restoration, notifications, background tasks) and the framework. iOS 17 or later.

- Bundle ids: `com.r0adkll.cassette` for TestFlight builds, `com.r0adkll.cassette.debug` for local
  debug builds, so both can be on a phone.
- Signing team `N6RWL4DC5S`. The version is `cassette.version`
  from gradle.properties, as on Android (`scripts/xcodegen` sets it); build numbers are the commit
  count, since every TestFlight upload needs a new one. The widgets and the watch app share both.
- Diagnostics: the capture log, raw adverts and hub tables as on Android ([0005](0005-diagnostics.md));
  the bundle goes to the share sheet. The watch panel shows the Apple Watch (see below).

## Distribution

TestFlight (internal testers), uploaded from CI with an App Store Connect API key, alongside the
Android builds' Drop uploads ([0008](0008-tooling.md)). CI's `iOS app` job builds the app and runs the
Simulator UI test on every push and pull request; `TestFlight` archives the Release build on pushes
to main and uploads it by exporting the archive (`ExportOptions.plist`), with automatic, cloud-managed
signing through the API key. It needs the app created in App Store Connect for `com.r0adkll.cassette`
and an API key (App Manager or Admin) in `APP_STORE_CONNECT_KEY_ID`, `APP_STORE_CONNECT_ISSUER_ID` and
`APP_STORE_CONNECT_KEY_BASE64`; without them it skips, as the Drop upload does without its keystore.

## Widgets (iteration 15, approved 2026-10-18)

The look of Android's widgets ([0012](0012-visual-direction.md), [0010](0010-product-details.md)) at
iOS's sizes, as a SwiftUI WidgetKit extension (`app/ios/Cassette/Widgets`) with Barlow bundled, so the
type is real text.

- **Home screen (H1 · Android ported):** small is the status block (bike, headline, the weakest
  component or when it was seen); medium is one vertical bar per component; large is a thick bar per
  component with the name and value inside. Each picks its bike in the widget's edit menu ("Bike";
  none picked, or a deleted bike, is the default bike). The small widget opens the bike; on medium and
  large each bar opens its component (`cassette://` links, [0011](0011-onboarding-alerts-and-data.md)).
  No Refresh: a widget can't run the 60-second scan.
- **All bikes:** medium is a row per bike tinted by its weakest status, as on Android; large adds each
  bike's components under its row. A row opens its bike.
- **Lock Screen (L2 · Bars and icon):** the system draws these in one vibrant tone, so the icon and
  words carry status. Rectangular: the bike, its status word and a mini bar per component. Circular:
  the status icon over a short word ("FD LOW", "GOOD", "OLD"; "CHARGE" for charge now, as the Wear
  complication says it). Inline: the icon and "FD LOW · ≈11%" in the system font.
- **Tinted and clear home screens:** iOS recolours everything; the status icon, the status word and
  the bar fills are marked accented, and the small widget drops its block fill.
- **StandBy (S2):** where iOS removes the background, the small widget sits on black with the
  headline and icon in the status colour. Night mode tints it all red, so the word carries status.
- Rejected: H2 (the block leading every size; rows get tight with an MTB's eight components), L1 (words
  and a gauge on the Lock Screen), S1 (the bright block kept in StandBy, by the bed).

**Data.** The app writes `widgets.json` to the App Group (`group.` + the app's bundle id, so debug and
TestFlight builds keep their own) whenever bike data changes, debounced like Android's widget updates,
then asks WidgetKit to reload. It comes from the same overviews as the Wear snapshot
([0013](0013-wear.md)), but worded: `:features:bikes:summary` needs Compose, which a widget extension
(about 30 MB of memory) can't carry, so `:surfaces:widgetkit` runs it in the app and writes each
bike's status and rows twice: as they read now, and as they'll read once the bike goes stale (14
days), with the time that happens. The extension only words the clock ("Seen 2 min ago", "Not seen in
16 days."), as `BikeSummary` does, and its timeline steps at the moments those words change. Each
write goes into the capture log ([0005](0005-diagnostics.md)).

## Live Activity (iteration 16, approved 2026-10-18)

While a refresh runs, a Live Activity shows it on the Lock Screen, in the Dynamic Island and in
StandBy, in the same widget extension (`RefreshActivityWidget`).

- **A · Tabletop card:** the tabletop refresh ([0014](0014-large-screens.md)) made small. A red
  ring counts the scan down with the time and "2 OF 5" inside, and a column per component fills in
  its status colour as it answers (waiting ones stay grey, "···"). The label says the bike and the
  step ("ROAD BIKE · READING"), and the line under it says what to do, who it's waiting for, or
  what it found ("Charge soon. Front derailleur at ≈11%."). When it ends, the ring becomes the green
  check (all answered), amber (some, "No answer from PM.") or grey (none, or Bluetooth turned off
  during the scan).
- **Dynamic Island:** compact is the ring and "2/5", minimal the ring; expanded adds the step and
  headline ("READING", "2 of 5.") beside the camera, then the columns and Stop and Add 60 s across
  its foot (`LiveActivityIntent` buttons, which run in the app's process). The corners beside the
  camera are too narrow for the bike's name and the buttons the mockup put there, and the island's
  height caps the ring at 52 pt. The island is always black, so it takes the dark palette.
- **StandBy:** full screen on black (`isActivityFullscreen`): a big ring beside the headline and
  tall columns. Night mode tints it red, so the words carry status.
- Taps open the bike (`cassette://bike/<id>`). Done (and the rest of the endings) stays on the Lock
  Screen for five minutes; Stop ends it at once.
- Rejected: B · live status block (a counting headline with bars; Done in the bike's status colour).

**State.** ActivityKit content updates, sent from the app; no push server, and not the App Group,
since an activity's view only redraws when ActivityKit hands it new content.
`CaptureController.latestRefresh` publishes every refresh, whichever surface started it (the bike
screen's on iOS), with Stop and Add 60 s reaching it from outside its collector. `:surfaces:widgetkit`
words it (`refreshActivityState`): the bike whose components answered most since the start (the
default bike until one has; a background discovery during the scan counts), its rows from
`BikeSummary`, the phase and the scan's end time. The views draw the countdown from that end time
(`Text(timerInterval:)` and a timer `ProgressView`), so the app sends an update only when a
component answers, Add 60 s moves the end, or the scan ends. Swift's `RefreshActivityController`
starts, updates and ends the activity from that JSON (a closure Kotlin calls, as the widgets'
reload is), marks it stale 15 s after the scan's end in case the app has gone, and ends any left
over at launch. Starts, ends and refusals go into the capture log ([0005](0005-diagnostics.md)).

Each step of a refresh says whether Bluetooth is on (`RefreshRun.bluetoothReady`), so a scan whose
Bluetooth goes off ends the activity as "Bluetooth is off". An ending state waits a second before it
goes to ActivityKit, so the bike data holding the last answer catches up (otherwise a refresh that
ended on its last answer read "4 of 5").

**Seeing it without a bike:** Diagnostics → Demo refresh (every build, with the other developer
tools, [0005](0005-diagnostics.md)) reloads the sample bike and runs a refresh in which it answers
without Bluetooth: the derailleurs at 6 s, the shifters at 15 s, the power meter at 30 s. It goes
through the same loop, `latestRefresh`, Stop and Add 60 s as a real one, and only ever writes to
the sample bike. The phone UI test ends with it and saves the island and the Lock Screen
(`a01` to `a05`). Checked on the Simulator (iOS 26.2): compact and expanded island, the Lock
Screen card with the system's timer ring, Done, and iOS's "Allow Live Activities" prompt.

The activity can only start in the foreground, so it starts when Refresh is tapped; with Bluetooth
already off the app says so itself and no activity starts. A background task keeps the scan going
for a while after the phone is locked. How long iOS allows it, and whether the live scan (no service
filter) hears anything with the screen locked, are unchecked until an iPhone and the bike; the
background `FE51` scan still records what it hears, and the activity counts those readings.

## Apple Watch (iteration 17, approved 2026-10-19)

A SwiftUI watchOS app with a widget extension for its complications, embedded in the iPhone app
(watchOS 10 or later). Like Wear ([0013](0013-wear.md)), the phone is the source: the watch never
scans.

- **App (B · a page per bike):** each bike is a page in its status colour (the status block as the
  whole screen: name, icon, headline, the weakest component and when it was seen), and the crown
  pages between bikes, the default bike first. A glass button opens the components as the large
  widget's thick bars; Refresh is the one solid button, so it keeps its contrast on any colour. An
  old reading is the outlined dark panel. Taps on a complication open its bike's page.
- **Complications (all five):** "Weakest battery" is the weakest component's level and code on a
  circular gauge, or its value in a corner with the gauge along the bezel. "Bike" is the status icon
  over its word in a circle ("CHARGE" for charge now), the status line, headline and a bar per
  component in a rectangle, and "FD LOW · ≈11%" inline. Each picks its bike in the face editor, the
  default bike unless one is chosen. Tinted faces recolour everything; the icon, the status word and
  the fills are the accented parts, so status still reads.
- **Smart Stack (S1 · status block):** the rectangular "Bike" in the Smart Stack is the Wear tile's
  card: the bike's colour behind its headline, then "FD ≈11% · 2 min ago". It rises in the stack
  when the bike was seen in the last two hours with something low (the pre-ride moment), and for a
  day while something needs charging now.
- Rejected: A (Wear's bike list, then a bike: one more tap to the status) and S2 (bars in the stack:
  more to read, less to see at a glance).

**Data.** The watch gets the widgets' snapshot, not Wear's: Wear's carries readings that the watch
words with `:features:bikes:summary`, which needs Compose and Kotlin, and neither runs on watchOS.
The widgets' snapshot is already worded, with its stale version, and the watch reuses the widgets'
Swift (`app/ios/Cassette/Snapshot`) to word the clock. `WatchPublisher` (`:surfaces:watchkit`) sends
one WatchConnectivity application context, which keeps only the latest and reaches the watch when it
next runs: the snapshot, and the refresh's state in Wear's shape (`WearRefresh`). The watch app keeps
the snapshot in its own App Group for the complications and reloads them. When what the
complications show changes (a bike's status or weakest value, the default bike), or after an hour,
the phone also pushes it as a complication update, which wakes the watch; iOS allows about 50 a day.

**Refresh.** The watch sends a message; WatchConnectivity wakes the iPhone app in the background if
needed, and it runs the shared 60-second refresh as Wear's request does, inside a background task.
The watch counts down from the phone's end time (60 s from the ask until the phone's state arrives).
"Can't reach your iPhone." when the phone isn't reachable; "Bluetooth is off on your iPhone." when
the phone says it can't scan. Whether a refresh started this way hears the bike while the phone is
locked waits for an iPhone, like the rest of background capture.

**Diagnostics.** The watch panel shows on iOS as "Apple Watch": whether one is paired and has
Cassette, the last context sent (or why not) and the last refresh the watch asked for, from the same
capture log events as Wear's.
